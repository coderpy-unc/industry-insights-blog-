Pre-engineered buildings — steel-framed warehouses, factories, logistics centres, cold storage facilities — represent the fastest-growing segment of industrial construction in India, and under-deck glass wool insulation is the most consistently underspecified element in these projects.

## Why PEB buildings dominate Indian industrial construction

Pre-engineered buildings have become the default format for new industrial construction in India for straightforward reasons: faster erection time (weeks versus months for conventional construction), lower structural steel weight, and the ability to expand modularly. A 50,000 sq ft warehouse can be erected in 8–10 weeks with a PEB system. The same building in conventional RCC construction takes 6–8 months.

The tradeoff is the roof — a profiled steel sheet, typically 0.47mm to 0.63mm thick, that provides no thermal or acoustic insulation whatsoever. The steel conducts heat directly from the sun into the space below. On a May afternoon in Delhi or Rajasthan, the underside of an uninsulated PEB roof reaches 55–65°C. The ambient temperature in the space below is 10–15°C above outdoor ambient — routinely 48–52°C at peak. Workers cannot function. Temperature-sensitive products cannot be stored. Machinery runs hot and fails early.

Glass wool under-deck insulation addresses this directly, cost-effectively, and permanently.

## What under-deck insulation does

Under-deck glass wool is installed on the underside of the roof purlins — between the steel structure and the roof sheet above. It is held in place by the roof sheet itself, by wire mesh, or by support clips depending on the installation method.

The glass wool creates a thermal break between the hot steel roof and the space below. The fibrous structure traps air pockets that resist heat transfer. A 50mm glass wool blanket at 24 kg/m³ reduces the radiant heat load from the roof by approximately 70–75%. A 75mm blanket reduces it by 80–85%.

The measurable outcomes in Indian conditions:

**Non-AC warehouses and factories** — interior temperature during peak summer is reduced by 6–8°C compared to an uninsulated building. Workers operate in 42–44°C rather than 50–52°C — a difference that is the boundary between functional and dangerous.

**AC-cooled buildings** — roof insulation reduces the cooling load by 30–40%, directly reducing chiller and AHU sizing at construction and electricity consumption through the building's life. A building spending ₹12 lakh per year on cooling can expect savings of ₹3.5–5 lakh annually from correct roof insulation — payback in under 3 years, savings for 25+ years.

## The acoustic benefit

This is consistently underestimated in PEB applications. A profiled steel roof in heavy rain is extremely loud — ambient noise levels of 75–80 dB(A) in the space below. For manufacturing facilities with precision operations, testing areas, or any application requiring verbal communication, this is a real operational problem.

Glass wool under-deck insulation reduces rain noise transmission by 15–20 dB(A), bringing typical heavy rain noise below 60 dB(A) — the practical threshold for normal verbal communication without strain. The same insulation that controls temperature also provides meaningful acoustic comfort, with no additional cost or installation complexity.

## The correct specification

The most common PEB insulation specification error is stating "glass wool insulation as required" without specifying density, thickness, or facing — leaving the contractor free to supply the cheapest available product.

A correct under-deck PEB insulation specification:

**Standard application (non-AC warehouse, factory):**
*"Glass wool insulation blanket, 24 kg/m³ density, 50mm thickness, with aluminium FSK facing on the upper surface, conforming to IS 8183:2024. Supplier to provide NABL-accredited test certificates for thermal conductivity (λ value) and density prior to delivery to site."*

**Enhanced thermal performance (AC building, cold storage approach zone):**
*"Glass wool insulation blanket, 32 kg/m³ density, 75mm thickness, with aluminium FSK facing on the upper surface, conforming to IS 8183:2024. Supplier to provide NABL-accredited test certificates for thermal conductivity and density prior to delivery."*

**Why FSK facing matters:** Aluminium foil-scrim-kraft (FSK) facing on the upper surface provides a radiant heat reflector that significantly improves the thermal performance of the insulation system. It also provides a vapour barrier that prevents condensation from forming on the underside of the roof in humid conditions — a major cause of corrosion in uninsulated or incorrectly specified PEB roofs.

## Wall insulation in PEB

Wall panels in PEB buildings are either single-skin profiled steel sheets or factory-made sandwich panels with insulation core. For single-skin walls in facilities where temperature or acoustic performance matters, site-applied glass wool between the outer skin and an inner liner sheet is the standard solution — typically 50mm at 24 kg/m³ for the wall application.

Sandwich panels — factory-fabricated with glass wool, rockwool, or PU foam core — are the premium option, with better surface finish and faster installation. Sipla supplies glass wool core for sandwich panel fabrication in addition to site-applied blanket insulation.

## IS 8183:2024 — what changed

IS 8183 was revised in 2024 for the first time since 1993. Key changes relevant to PEB specifications: tighter dimensional tolerances, expanded thermal conductivity specifications, and reduced sulphur content limits. Any specification written after 2024 should reference IS 8183:2024, not the earlier edition.
